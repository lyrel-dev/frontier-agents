# Editorial standard

## Explanatory sequence

Introduce the agent and its resource model before listing functions. Explain integration direction and authorization before describing data use. Explain how knowledge is produced, retained, selected, corrected, and removed before applying it to a use case.

## Prose and headings

Use descriptive sentence-case noun phrases, as in a technical book. Begin a section with the idea and its relationship to the preceding topic. Use procedures and tables where they answer configuration or comparison questions; do not substitute labeled fragments for an explanation. Catalog records are narrated situations, work, and interpretations with reference fields as a supplement.

## Diagrams

Provide named resources, directional relationships, a caption, accessible title and description, and linked source sections. Distinguish logical models from undisclosed implementation. Do not draw a shared runtime, memory store, or permission model without evidence.

## Product claims

Link material product behavior to official documentation. Separate native behavior, configured integrations, team-composed procedures, and synthetic evidence. Availability applies to the specific feature. Memory retrieval is not automatically model training; retention is not automatically backup and recovery.

## Presentations

Maintain HTML decks with visible chapter membership, substantive diagrams or comparisons, source-linked notes, and explicit exercise budgets. Choose content form according to the subject; no arbitrary required number of rows, cards, or procedure steps defines quality.

## Verification

Check source mapping and content consistency separately from HTML behavior. Check actual rendering, navigation, filters, figure accessibility, slide timing, and print restoration. Report what was tested and what was not. No live-service result may be inferred from a document build.
